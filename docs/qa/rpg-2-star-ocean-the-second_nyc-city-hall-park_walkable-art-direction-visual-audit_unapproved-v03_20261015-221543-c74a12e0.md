# City Hall Park walkable composition v03 — visual audit

The v03 image is a separate nonruntime art-direction study, not a replacement for the current game scene. It was generated by editing the earlier geography-corrected v02 reference.

## Observed improvement

- City Hall, its cupola, and the fountain remain visible as distinct landmarks.
- The southern and central paths are broader and easier to trace than in v02. A single foreground adventurer gives an approximate visual scale.
- Street, lawn, garden, and discreet science fixtures form recognizable zones without changing the science-circle access rule.

## Still unacceptable as runtime art

- Output is 1672×941 continuous-colour generated illustration, not a 1280×720 hand-authored Pixelorama scene with editable layers and consistent pixel clusters.
- The foreground figure is roughly hero-scale, but the actual approved 48-logical-pixel female south idle frame was not composited or checked in Godot. The generated figure is not a canon character sprite.
- Tree crowns, flowers, small posts, and numerous tiny figures still make parts of the center visually busy. Exact walkable width, occlusion, collision, and interactions are unverified.
- The building's detailed geometry and street furnishings are a design interpretation. Use the official City Hall site plan cited in the v01 visual audit when authoring a real scene.
- The generated building is much too monumental for a literal City Hall model: the broad multi-storey frontage, roof terraces and repeated bays should not be traced. The official city description identifies a central pavilion, two projecting wings, a columned entrance portico and a domed tower. Keep those identifying forms in the pixel-art pilot, and treat future additions as 2537 fiction.

## Primary location anchors checked 2026-10-16

- [NYC Parks' City Hall Park page](https://nycgovparks.org/parks/city-hall-park) identifies Broadway, Park Row and Chambers Street as its bounding street references and confirms the park's civic setting.
- [NYC's City Hall page](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dcas/business/dcasmanagedbuildings/city-hall.page) places the building within City Hall Park and describes its central pavilion, projecting wings, entrance portico and domed tower. These references support the location and recognisable civic building, not the exact 2537 furniture, technology or path layout in the concept image.
